About

I am a Ph.D. candidate in Computer Science at Wayne State University, advised by Dr. Zheng Dong. My research builds efficient, adaptive, and time-critical computing systems for autonomous and software-defined vehicles (SDVs).

Modern vehicles must run increasingly complex perception workloads under strict latency deadlines, limited onboard resources, and changing network conditions. My work addresses this through inference acceleration, edge–vehicle collaborative computing and task offloading, and scene context-aware model selection, so that a vehicle's computation and communication decisions adapt to scene complexity, timing constraints, and available resources. My work appears in venues including the IEEE Internet of Things Journal, ACM Transactions on Embedded Computing Systems, and IEEE MOST.

I received my B.S. in Computer Science and Engineering from Khulna University of Engineering & Technology (KUET), Bangladesh. I am currently a Rumble Fellow at Wayne State University.

Research

A self-driving car has to understand its surroundings within milliseconds, on modest onboard hardware, through rain, darkness, and heavy traffic, and often over an unreliable wireless link. I design perception systems for software-defined vehicles that stay fast, efficient, and accurate under all of these pressures. The thread connecting my work is runtime orchestration: giving the vehicle the ability to answer, frame by frame,

How lean can the model be? Run locally or offload? What is worth computing? Which detector fits this scene? Onboard, edge, or cloud?

First-Author Published Research

Optimized Inference Workflows

Does the way a detector is deployed matter as much as the detector itself? I built and compared PyTorch- and TensorFlow-based execution pipelines, including direct TensorRT, ONNX-to-TensorRT, and Torch-TensorRT conversion, at FP32, FP16, and INT8 precision on COCO, KITTI, and BDD100K. FP16 gave a better balance of speed and accuracy than INT8.

The PyTorch–ONNX–TensorRT pipeline at FP16 delivered about 7× higher throughput and 6× faster inference while largely preserving accuracy.

MOST’24

IEEE IoTJ

Efficient Perception Inference

Faster pipelines alone cannot shrink an oversized network, so I paired them with model compression using three pruning techniques: layer pruning (dropping low-importance layers or channels), soft pruning (suppressing weak weights while letting them recover during training), and group pruning (removing structurally linked parameter groups). Fine-tuning and knowledge distillation recover lost accuracy, and each pruned model was tested across three optimized workflows and three precisions.

Up to 18× faster inference, 16.5× higher throughput, and 30% lower GPU/memory use with minimal accuracy loss.

IoTJ’24

IEEE IoTJ

Scene-Aware Detector Selection

After benchmarking 21 detectors in fog, snow, rain, night, highway, and city driving, I found no model wins everywhere. SAVER has a vision-language model reason about each scene and its risk, sets a matching latency budget, and uses reinforcement-learning memory to keep its choices steady over time.

Higher accuracy within latency budgets than seven routing baselines across three public driving datasets.
